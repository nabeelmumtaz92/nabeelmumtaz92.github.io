UX Case Study · iOS Customer App

Return It. We'll pick it up.

Designing the customer app for an on-demand returns service — doorstep pickup of online returns, delivered back to the store by a local driver. I founded the company, designed the app in Figma, and I'm engineering it end to end: a team of one.

The problem

Returning an online order is a chore nobody asked for.

Printing a label, finding a box, repackaging the item, driving to a drop-off point, waiting in line — the return is often more work than the purchase. For busy households the friction is high enough that unwanted items sit in closets for weeks.

Return It removes the entire errand. A customer books a pickup, hands the item to a driver at their door, and the driver delivers it to the store. No printing, no packaging, no trip. The design challenge: make a logistics service feel as effortless as the pitch — warm, trustworthy, and obvious at first glance.

The approach

Designed the way the business runs: one person, end to end.

As founder, designer, and engineer, I don't hand designs over a wall — I design what I can ship. I prototyped the customer app in Figma Make to iterate on flows quickly, then built the "House Call" design system around the feeling the brand needs: a friendly neighbor knocking on your door, not a freight company.

Warm stone-paper backgrounds, pine-green ink, dark action cards, and rounded, plain-spoken copy. Every screen answers one question within seconds: what happens next, and what does it cost. Pricing is never more than a glance away — trust is the product.

Designing as the engineer changes the designs. Floating banners respect the tab bar's safe area, the order stepper maps 1:1 to backend order states, and every price shown on screen comes from the same pricing model the app charges against. Nothing in these screens is decorative fiction.

The screens

Four finished screens, one continuous journey.

Welcome → sign in → home → orders. Each screen does exactly one job. Screenshots taken directly from the Figma prototype.

01

Welcome

The front door. Headline "Return It. We'll Pick It Up." with the subhead "We handle the rest." — then three scannable tag chips (Free Pickup, Same-Day, Tracked) above a dark Sign In button and an outline Sign Up button.

Design decisionChips instead of sentences. The three value props read in a single glance — no paragraph to parse before the user decides whether to continue. The screen sells the outcome and defers everything else.
02

Sign In

"Welcome Back — sign in to your account." Email and password fields with a show/hide toggle, "Forgot password?", a dark primary Sign In button, then social options: Sign in with Google and Sign in with Apple.

Design decisionApple and Google sign-in sit below the fold of the primary button, not beside it — email stays the main path (it's what the driver communications use), while social login is a genuine shortcut, not a dark pattern.
03

Home

"Welcome back, Jordan — ready when you are." A dark Schedule Pickup card as the primary action, a ReturnPass · $9.99/mo membership promo (members pay $2 per return instead of $6), a 3-step "How It Works" explainer, and a floating "View Current Return — Target · Step 2 of 4" banner over a Home / Book / Orders / Profile tab bar.

Design decisionThe active return never leaves the screen — the floating banner keeps live order status one tap away from anywhere on Home. Pricing ($2 vs $6) is stated inside the promo itself, so the membership decision needs no second screen.
04

Orders

"My Orders" — 12 total orders with filter tabs: All (12), Active (3), Completed (8), Cancelled (1). Each card shows the store, order number, price, pickup time, status pill, and driver name — e.g. Target #RI-2024-7823 · $9.50 · Today 2:30 PM · In Transit · Driver: Marcus J.

Design decisionStatus pills use plain words (In Transit, Assigned, Pending) instead of internal logistics codes. The driver gets a name on the card because accountability is the feature — you know exactly who has your package.

Design system

"House Call" — warm paper, pine ink.

Named for what the service is: someone coming to your house to help. The system runs across every screen — stone-paper surfaces, pine-green ink, dark action cards, one rounded voice.

Stone Paper~ #F6F1E6 · surfaces
Deep Stone~ #ECE4D1 · cards
Pine~ #1E4D38 · brand accents
Ink Card~ #1B2B22 · primary actions
Ink Text~ #1C2420 · type
💬 Plain-spoken copy"We'll take it from here." No logistics jargon anywhere the customer can see it.
💲 Price always visible$9.50 per pickup, $2 vs $6 for members — stated on the screen where the decision happens.
🟢 Status in plain wordsIn Transit, Assigned, Pending. If a customer can't read it aloud, it doesn't ship.
🖐️ One job per screenWelcome sells, Home starts, Orders tracks. No screen does two things.
